## Project summary

RAÍZ is a tourist-payments network on Stellar in which every payment sends a configurable share, the Tip Barrio (2% by default, 200 basis points; source: README.md §1 and §4), to a community fund governed by the neighborhood's residents through soulbound, non-transferable tokens. The visitor pays the merchant in USDC, the Pool contract routes the tip to the neighborhood's fund, residents propose and vote on how to reinvest it (quorum 30% of residents plus simple majority; source: README.md §3b), and a Treasury contract executes approved proposals trustlessly, with nobody holding the key to the fund; the idle fund earns yield in Blend v2 through our own YieldAdapter contract. All state is on-chain: five Soroban contracts (Pool, Governance, Treasury, Rewards, YieldAdapter) deployed on Stellar testnet on 2026-07-31 (source: deployments.json), a native Android app (Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, kmp-stellar-sdk 1.6.0) at version 0.4.0 that ships zero credentials (source: https://github.com/JuanWimmin/Protocolo_Raiz/releases/tag/v0.4.0 and the public verify-apk CI), an open-source relayer for the four admin operations (https://github.com/JuanWimmin/raiz-relayer, live at https://raiz-relayer.fly.dev/v1/health), SEP-10 + SEP-24 deposits for seed and passkey wallets, and eight community-fund executions linked to their real transaction hashes (source: docs/evidencia_sow/README.en.md). Public site: https://raizapp.xyz.

At HackMeridian 2026 (Lisbon, October 25–26; builder track Scale, thematic track Stellar Unlocked) we built deferred payments over a device swarm for territories without signal. The visitor signs, with no connectivity, the SorobanAuthorizationEntry of Pool.pay_merchant and hands it over as a QR "envelope" (in Spanish, "sobre"); any device with signal — the merchant, a resident carrying it, a public web page, or the payer's own phone when it reconnects — submits it as the transaction's source and fee payer, and the network guarantees uniqueness (a nonce consumed on-chain) and expiration (a ledger number). No new contract was deployed: everything calls Pool.pay_merchant exactly as deployed (source: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md, header). Four settlement paths exist and each has a testnet transaction whose source account is not the payer: a Kotlin integration test, a terminal gateway, the public web gateway https://raizapp.xyz/pasarela.html (exercised end to end in headless Chrome; a manual run from a phone browser is still pending) and the app itself acting as carrier, on an Android emulator (source: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md §2 and §4). Design, math and threat model: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md; architecture: docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13; evidence package with a five-minute verification guide: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md.

## Problem and target users

RAÍZ addresses extractive tourism: the value created by the residents, artisans and corner shops of an attractive neighborhood is captured by platforms and chains, and the people who create the attraction decide nothing about how it is reinvested (source: README.md §1). The problem we attacked at the hackathon is narrower and concrete. In tourist territories with intermittent coverage — rural areas, visitors without a local SIM — a payment can fail for the simplest reason: there is no signal; our own proposal names this as the pain to solve, and we have no frequency data to quote (source: docs/NuevaPropuesta/propuesta_raiz_ahorro_enjambre.md §5.3). Our demo script opens with the scene that frames the problem for us: a rural road ("vereda") without coverage and an artisan in Cota who lost a sale because the card terminal would not come on — a narrative line of our own script, not a quote from an interview (source: docs/Pago diferido por enjambre.md §07, "La toma de la demo"; docs/PLAN_CLAUDE_CODE_HACKMERIDIAN.md, B5). Today's online payment flow needs the network at the exact moment of paying for four reasons: the payer's sequence number, the Soroban simulation that produces footprint and resource fees, the authorization nonce that the simulation returns, and XLM for the fee (source: docs/Pago diferido por enjambre.md §02). Pre-signing a complete transaction does not help, because it pins the payer's sequence number and still lacks the simulation; payment channels, TEE-based offline wallets and chained credentials need per-pair contracts, Intel SGX or a central issuer, none of which fits an ordinary phone or Stellar as it is today (source: comparison table in docs/Pago diferido por enjambre.md §02).

Target users are, first, visitors (tourists, often without a local SIM) who want to pay a merchant in USDC and leave the 2% to the neighborhood; second, merchants and artisans in territories with intermittent coverage, who need to accept a payment now and have it settled when any device reaches the network, with an honest view of what they hold; third, residents, who govern the community fund with soulbound tokens and who, in the swarm, can carry envelopes to where there is signal; and fourth, the community organizations that need the fund to be auditable by anyone. Customer discovery is early and we say so: the project has recorded interviews with merchants and residents (Drive folder linked from README.md), three to date (source: docs/evidencia_sow/closeout_sprint1_respuestas_EN.md, "General Comments and Outcomes"), and the next Instawards sprint — today a draft Statement of Work, not an approved commitment — would pair development with structured field validation with merchants, residents and visitors in a sustainable-tourism territory (source: docs/SOW2_RAIZ_borrador.docx, draft, not versioned).

## Value proposition

For the visitor: pay in USDC with an automatic 2% Tip Barrio to the neighborhood, earn non-transferable points (1 point per 0.01 USDC of tip; source: README.md §4) redeemable for local crafts, and now start a payment even without signal — a deferred payment: the app signs an envelope ("sobre") that the merchant verifies locally and that anyone settles later; the payer's own phone also settles it on its own when it regains signal (source: docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.5). For the merchant: receive the sale in USDC, see honest states — "en tránsito" ("in transit") is a signed promise, not confirmed money, and "Cobrado" ("collected") appears only with a transaction hash whose authorization is that envelope — and accept deferred payments under a per-payment cap of 20 USDC by default, editable (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §6; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.5). For the neighborhood: a fund that grows with every payment, earns yield in Blend v2 and is spent only through proposals that pass quorum and majority, executed by a contract nobody holds the key to, with every payment, vote and execution visible as an on-chain event (source: README.md §3b and §7). For the Stellar ecosystem: a reusable pattern — a Soroban authorization entry signed offline and submitted by any fee-paying account — that works with the contracts as deployed, needs no new contract, no trusted gateway and no server of our own: the public gateway is a static page, landing/pasarela.html, published at https://raizapp.xyz/pasarela.html (source: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md §1, B3). What we do not promise: offline finality. The double-spend window is bounded, not eliminated, and we state it in the UI, the README and the pitch with the same words (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §0 and §3.2).

## Technical architecture of the new work

Roles (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §1; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.2). The payer — the visitor, with a seed wallet in v0 — builds and signs offline the SorobanAuthorizationEntry of Pool.pay_merchant(tourist, merchant, amount, tip_bps) and exports it as an envelope by QR or as text. The receiver — COBRO when the merchant argument is this wallet, PORTADOR otherwise — verifies it with no network: RAÍZ prefix and valid XDR, signer is a G account, the root is pay_merchant of our Pool with four arguments, the signer is the payer of the tree, the tree rebuilt with our fee_bps (50) is byte-identical, and the ed25519 signature is valid for the testnet network id; it then stores it "en tránsito" and, if it is a COBRO, applies the per-payment cap. The gateway — any G account with XLM and signal: the app with a seed wallet, the public web page, the terminal script, or in v1 the relayer — rebuilds a transaction with its own account as source, runs an enforce-mode simulation, pays the fee, submits and returns the hash. The gateway does not need to be trusted: the payer's signature fixes the entire invocation tree, and the host rejects any change with Error(Auth, InvalidAction).

What is signed (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §2 and §2.1; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.3). The payer signs the SHA-256 of the HashIdPreimage of type ENVELOPE_TYPE_SOROBAN_AUTHORIZATION (network_id, nonce, signature_expiration_ledger, invocation). The invocation is the whole tree: the root call plus up to three transfer sub-invocations of the fund's USDC Stellar Asset Contract — merchant receives amount − fee, the Pool receives the tip, the admin receives the fee — mirroring contracts/pool/src/lib.rs with the contract's integer arithmetic (i128, truncation); Rewards.accrue_points is authorized by the Pool itself and is not in the tourist's tree. The fixture's --verify-tree mode checks against an unsigned simulation that the hand-built tree equals the one the host emits. Credentials are legacy address credentials (preimage type 9), the only kind kmp-stellar-sdk 1.6.0 knows; Protocol 29 still accepts them and emits CAP-71 V2 credentials only on request (measured 2026-10-25). The signed entry is 912 bytes of XDR; with the raiz:sobre:1: prefix and base64url without padding it is 1 229 characters, QR version 29 with correction M (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §3.4 and §5).

Nonce and expiration (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §2.2 and §3.1; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.3). The nonce is 64 random bits from the phone's CSPRNG; the host stores the consumed nonce as a temporary ledger entry under the payer's account, alive until the expiration ledger, so a second submission fails with Error(Auth, ExistingValue), and a getLedgerEntries read of that key proves that a transaction with that envelope executed without trusting any gateway. The expiration ledger is estimated without network from the last anchor (L0, t0) recorded whenever the phone had signal, using the monotonic clock and never the wall clock: L̂ = L0 + floor((t − t0) / τ) with τ = 5 s (measured 5.000 s), and E = L̂ + W with W = T_des / τ_min + Δ_max · (1/τ_min − 1/τ) = 9 600 + 1 920 = 11 520 ledgers, which guarantees 12 hours in the worst case (network closing at 4.5 s after 24 hours without signal) and gives about 16 hours at the measured close; the UI shows the guaranteed window as "Válido ~12 h (hasta ~hh:mm, estimado)". Without a valid anchor — none, from another boot, older than 24 hours, or with a wall-clock jump above 15 minutes — the app does not sign.

Components (source: docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.4 and §13.6). In the Android app, package data/deferred/: AuthTreeBuilder and DeferredAuthSigner (tree and signature, pure, byte-tested), SobreCodec and SobreValidator (envelope format and network-free verification), LedgerMath and LedgerEstimator, DeferredStore and DeferredPolicy (the payer's envelopes and their states), ReceivedSobre, ReceivedSobreVerifier, ReceivedSobrePolicy and ReceivedSobreStore (the receiver's envelopes, roles and anti-fraud rules), SobreSettler (the single orchestrator: locks per envelope and per source account, pre-send journal, resume by hash, backoff, started on connectivity transitions) and SobreSubmit/SobreErrors (classification of the network's answers); in data/stellar/, SorobanClient.submitSobre, nonceConsumed, sobrePaymentEvents and lookupSobreHash with the pure helpers EventsPaging and SobreLookup; data/network/ConnectivityMonitor; data/local/MerchantCache (merchants offline come from the cache filled while online). UI: Pay ("Firmar sin señal"), the envelope screen, "Mis sobres", "Recibir sobre" and Cobros. Web: landing/pasarela.html, static, applying the same verification rules. Scripts: gen-auth-fixture.mjs, pasarela_cli.mjs, deferred_envelope_demo.mjs, pasarela_web_e2e.mjs.

Closure rules, decided after an adversarial review (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §6; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.5). The network is the only source of truth. A received envelope is "Cobrado" only with a transaction hash whose authorization is that envelope; a consumed nonce without a hash stays in transit "looking for the receipt"; the nonce seen in another payment, or expired without a hash after having been seen consumed, is rejected as possible fraud; only Error(Auth, InvalidAction) and a reused nonce are definitive, while funds, trustline, unverified merchant, source-account result codes and network problems postpone with a visible reason; before declaring an envelope expired, its hash is searched in the Pool's payment events since the anchor, because the nonce entry is temporary and disappears when it expires. Fee caps: 100 XLM for the payer's own envelopes, 2 XLM for the automatic settlement of received ones and 100 XLM with "Liquidar ahora". A COBRO is never accepted without a ledger reference (real, or estimated with a 360-ledger margin plus drift by the anchor's age) nor above the per-payment cap (20 USDC by default); a PORTADOR is accepted with a warning that validity was not checked. Passkey wallets (C…) do not sign offline nor submit from the app in v0: they copy the envelope and open the public gateway with the #sobre= fragment.

Contracts and accounts used, all on testnet and all pre-existing (source: deployments.json; docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md §6): Pool CD775D33SPEO3BTAZIEQTQGN6HERTR5YNEQOZWWKXLDKLJ2B34LCKBE2 (root of the signed tree), USDC Stellar Asset Contract of Blend's testnet USDC CAQCFVLOBK5GIULPNZRGATJJMIZL5BSP7X5YJVMGCPTUEPFM4AVSRCJU (issuer GATALTGTWIOT6BUDBCZM3Q4OQ4BO2COLOAZ7IYSKPLC2PMSOPPGF5V56), protocol admin GBLS7PL5Y65DHQIPMJO6HVQLX4FXEEHQDWHGSBUTGT4V6ZV2IOACYC2P (receives the 0.5% fee). The admin key is never needed by the deferred payment: the payer signs, anyone submits.

## Stellar features used

The core of the new work is Soroban's authorization model: a SorobanAuthorizationEntry with address credentials (SOROBAN_CREDENTIALS_ADDRESS) signed offline over the ENVELOPE_TYPE_SOROBAN_AUTHORIZATION preimage, which covers the whole invocation tree — the root pay_merchant call plus the USDC Stellar Asset Contract transfer sub-invocations — and not the transaction or the footprint; that separation of intention from submission is what lets a visitor sign with no network and someone else submit later, and it is why we entered the Stellar Unlocked track (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §2; docs/Pago diferido por enjambre.md §02 and §04). The entry's nonce and signature_expiration_ledger provide network-enforced uniqueness and expiry: the consumed nonce lives as a temporary ledger entry until the expiration ledger, replay fails with Error(Auth, ExistingValue), an expired entry fails with Error(Auth, InvalidInput), and the network bounds E − L to max_entry_ttl − 1 = 3 110 399 ledgers, all measured on testnet under Protocol 29 (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §3.1). The gateway submits an invokeHostFunction operation from a different source account that pays the fee, with auth = [entry]; it simulates in enforcing mode before signing, because the authorization is already present, and assembleTransaction (JavaScript) or prepareTransaction (Kotlin) preserve the attached authorization (source: docs/PLAN_CLAUDE_CODE_HACKMERIDIAN.md, B1 result; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.6). RPC methods used: getLatestLedger as the estimator's anchor; getLedgerEntries on the LedgerKeyNonce to prove consumption; getTransaction to compare the authorization of a hash with the envelope; getEvents on the Pool's payment events, paginated by cursor, to find a settlement before declaring expiration (source: docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.4). Protocol 29 behavior measured: legacy credentials accepted, CAP-71 V2 credentials emitted only when requested (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §2). Classic pieces: the Stellar Asset Contract of USDC for the three transfers, friendbot for disposable gateway accounts and Horizon for balances and transaction reads (source: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md §2 and §6). SDKs: kmp-stellar-sdk 1.6.0 (Soneso) in the app and @stellar/stellar-sdk 17.0.1 in the gateways, verified byte for byte against each other through the fixture (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §7b).

Prior work in the same product, on which the new work stands: five Soroban contracts in Rust with soroban-sdk 26.1.1, including cross-contract calls (Pool → Rewards, Pool → YieldAdapter, Treasury → Governance and Pool) and on-chain events for the transparency dashboard; passkey smart accounts (secp256r1, OpenZeppelin smart account via Soneso's OZSmartAccountKit) alongside SEP-05 seed wallets; SEP-1, SEP-10 and SEP-24 against the SDF test anchor, with PathPaymentStrictSend for the conversion between the anchor's USDC and the fund's USDC; Blend v2 as the yield source behind our YieldAdapter; and Stellar Expert links for every transaction shown in the app and on the landing (source: README.md §2, §4 and §5).

## Sustainability model

The sustainability model is under evaluation and we present it as such, without figures. One rule is fixed and not up for discussion: the 2% Tip Barrio is not touched — it goes entirely to the community fund governed by the residents (source: README.md §1; docs/PLAN_CLAUDE_CODE_HACKMERIDIAN.md, B6). What exists on-chain today is a protocol fee parameter, protocol_fee_bps = 50 (0.5%), paid to the protocol admin account on every payment (source: deployments.json; README.md §7); we have recorded as a debt that the deployed Pool deducts that fee from the merchant's amount (to_merchant = amount − fee), in tension with the principle that the merchant receives the full sale, to be corrected in the v2 contracts, not during the event (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §2.1). The options under evaluation for the service itself are, in the words of our plan, a charge borne by the visitor and voluntary support (source: docs/PLAN_CLAUDE_CODE_HACKMERIDIAN.md, B6); the project's broader proposal also describes letting the protocol fee fund the neighborhood's own infrastructure rather than an admin (source: docs/NuevaPropuesta/propuesta_raiz_ahorro_enjambre.md §5.5). The deferred payment adds a specific open question: who pays the gateway's XLM fee — a volunteer gateway, the merchant, or the relayer — and it cannot be answered from testnet costs: the first pay_merchant of a tourist in a neighborhood cost 28.65 XLM there because it creates two persistent entries and pays their initial rent, while the following ones cost about 0.0023 XLM; demo gateways are funded by friendbot and we promise no cost figure until it is measured on mainnet (source: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md §2, fee note; docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §3.1). We have no revenue data and give no projections; the field validation proposed for the next Instawards sprint — today a draft Statement of Work, not an approved commitment — is designed to produce an evidence-based recommendation for the revenue model (source: docs/SOW2_RAIZ_borrador.docx, deliverable D4; draft, not versioned).

## Future goals and next steps

The immediate next steps are the v1 items our specification defers to the next sprint (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §7 and §10; docs/ARQUITECTURA_TECNICA.md §13.8). First, the prefunded territory pocket: a contract in which the visitor locks an amount while online so that envelopes are drawn against it, bounding the merchants' aggregate loss to max(0, Σ amounts − pocket) (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §3.2). Second, hardware Key Attestation of an Android Keystore key, so that a synced passkey or a second device cannot sign envelopes (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §4). Third, carriers over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct, with the gossip protocol deduplicated by (payer, nonce) sketched in docs/Pago diferido por enjambre.md §A5; in v0 envelopes travel by QR and copy/paste only. Fourth, the encrypted envelope (X25519 to the gateway's key), so that a carrier cannot read who paid whom — a decision to take with the community, because sealing it to the relayer would remove "any phone with signal works" (source: docs/Pago diferido por enjambre.md §A5; docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §4). Fifth, the relayer as a gateway (POST /v1/deferred-payment with a structured intention plus the authorization entry) and SEP-45 wallet authentication in the relayer, which with direct passkey signing removes the deposit-account bridge and lets passkey smart accounts sign and settle envelopes from the app; today they settle through the public page (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §7; docs/PLAN_CLAUDE_CODE_SOW.md, WP7; README.md §9). Also planned: iOS via KMP, the territory's durable history beyond the RPC's roughly seven-day event retention through the raiz-memory index (source: docs/pago_diferido_v0.md §10), and the debts recorded this weekend — merchant amount net of the protocol fee and TTL management on touch in the v2 contracts, CAP-71 V2 credentials when the Kotlin SDK ships them, and testing of the payer, carrier and COBRO flows on physical devices, since this round ran on an emulator and in headless Chrome (source: docs/evidencia_hackmeridian/README.md §4).

Beyond the deferred payment, the canonical roadmap is F1–F6 of the community-savings proposal (source: docs/NuevaPropuesta/propuesta_raiz_ahorro_enjambre.md §8; README.md §9): F1, yield directly in Blend v2 through the YieldAdapter, done on 2026-07-31; F2, the Cadena de Barrio, a soulbound rotating savings circle (savings_circle), currently paused until the evidence commitments are delivered (source: CLAUDE.md); F3, swarm custody — the neighborhood's fund as a smart account whose signers are residents' passkeys with policies — and neighbor attestation of residency, which removes the single admin key and the mock KYC; F4, savings goals, challenges and a no-loss lottery; F5, the frontier swarm — mesh store-and-forward, light verification on the phone and DePIN-style rewards; F6, ZK secret voting. The mainnet prerequisites are stated in README.md §9: admin custody without a single key (a 2-of-3 multisig script is prepared), production anchors (SEP-38, withdrawal, SEP-45, fiat↔USDC anchors), an own domain for the passkey rpId, SEP-12 residency, and an audit. Funding path on record: the proposal positions F3 and F5 as Stellar Community Fund projects (source: docs/NuevaPropuesta/propuesta_raiz_ahorro_enjambre.md §8), and the next Instawards sprint is today a draft Statement of Work that pairs development with field validation in a real territory. In fairness to that document: the draft classifies the Enjambre layer as a design note only for its sprint; the hackathon moved it ahead to v0, and we will propose updating that scope (source: docs/SOW2_RAIZ_borrador.docx, "Out-of-Scope" and Annex A.3; draft, not versioned).
